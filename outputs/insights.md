# Key Insights

1. **The ad works.** Users who saw the ad converted at **2.55%**, vs **1.79%** for the control group who saw a public service announcement.
2. **Lift:** that is **43.1% more conversions** (95% confidence range 30.0% to 57.5%), or +0.77 percentage points. A 20,000-run simulation (bootstrap) agrees: 30.4% to 57.9%.
3. **Not luck:** a two-proportion z-test gives z = 7.37 and p = 1.7e-13, far below the 0.05 threshold. A chi-square test gives the same answer (p = 1.7e-13).
4. **Big enough sample:** with 23,524 control users the test could detect a lift as small as 13.8%; the observed 43.1% is far above that.
5. **Robust:** removing the top 0.1% of users by ads seen leaves the result unchanged (lift 42.8%, p = 2.7e-13).
6. **Business impact:** the ad produced about **4,343 extra conversions** among 564,577 users. At an assumed ₹1,000 per conversion that is about **₹43.4 lakh** of extra value.
7. **By day:** the lift is clearest on Tuesday (+111%) and weakest on Thursday (+7%). Only 3 of 7 days (Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday) pass the stricter per-segment test, so treat day differences as hints to test next, not proven facts.
8. **The ad needs repetition:** conversion rises with ads seen in BOTH groups (0.31% at 1-9 ads to about 16% at 200+), because active users simply see more of everything. The ad's real effect is the GAP between the groups: it is significant only for 20-49, 50-99, 100-199 ads, peaking at 50-99 ads (+105%). For 1-9, 10-19, 200+ ads there is no proven difference.

**Decision: launch the ad campaign.** The lift is statistically significant (p < 0.05) and large enough to matter. Next, run a follow-up test on ad frequency (the effect appears only after about 20 views) and on day targeting (the lift looks strongest early in the week).

_Value per conversion (₹1,000) is an assumption; the dataset has no revenue._
